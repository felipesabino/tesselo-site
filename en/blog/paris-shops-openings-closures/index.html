Paris, 2009–2026: every shop that opens, every shop that closes

An animated map of 120,000 Paris shops, month by month, drawn from INSEE’s SIRENE register. What it shows, where the data comes from and where it stops.

Each dot on this map is a Paris shop listed in SIRENE, the business register kept by INSEE, the French statistics office. Press Play: openings appear in green and closures fade out in red, from January 2009 to October 2026. The menu limits the map to one type of shop. Under the map, the chart counts openings and closures over the previous twelve months; the vertical line follows the film’s date, and a click on the chart takes the map there. The light band marks the Covid period, from the first lockdown (March 2020) to the reopening of shops (May 2021).

What it shows

Over eighteen years, the register holds about 88,000 openings and 59,700 closures for the 28 types of shop we follow. Three signals stand out.

  • 2020 froze the map, in both directions. 4,189 openings against 5,424 in 2019, 23% fewer. Closures fell too: 3,378 against 3,782.
  • Closures have been at their highest since 2023. 3,992 in 2023, 4,184 in 2024, 4,072 in 2025: the three highest years of the series. Openings are still more numerous (5,434 in 2025).
  • Types of shop do not follow the same slope. The chart compares three years of openings at the start and at the end of the period.

Clothing shop openings have more than halved (1,755, then 814) while closures fell much less (1,360, then 1,177): this type now closes more than it opens. Fast food now opens almost as much as sit-down restaurants (3,422 against 3,489). Pick “Clothing” then “Fast food” in the map’s menu to see where it happens.

Where the data comes from

The SIRENE register is the official register of French businesses, kept by INSEE. It is public, free and reusable under Licence Ouverte 2.0.

  • One line per establishment, meaning per place of business: a brand with ten shops has ten lines.
  • What each line holds: the address, the activity code (bakery, hairdressing, restaurant…), the creation date, the state (open or closed) and the date the state last changed.
  • Updated daily through the API, with a full file for all of France on the 1st of each month (44 million establishments, open and closed).
  • Closed establishments stay in the file. That is what lets you go back in time: the creation date gives the opening, and the date the state became “closed” gives the closure.

What the register does not say

An animated map looks exact. Here is where that stops.

  • Closures arrive late, and sometimes never. A shop that shuts without filing anything stays “open” in the register. The number of open shops under the date is therefore an upper bound, and it swells over time. Dated openings and closures are more reliable than that total.
  • A takeover or a move counts twice. When a shop changes operator or address, the old establishment closes and a new one opens. The bakery on your street may have “closed” three times without ever stopping selling bread.
  • The activity code is declared by the operator. It can be vague or out of date. Some trades have no code of their own: vape shops share theirs with a dozen other kinds of shop, and are not on this map.
  • Before 2009 the history is incomplete. Closures before 2008 are filed under an older list of activity codes, so the map starts in January 2009.
  • Business address providers are left out. At some addresses, hundreds of unrelated companies are registered without trading there. We leave out the addresses that gather at least twenty establishments of ten or more different types, with almost no chain brand: 97 addresses, 6,326 establishments. Shopping centres and stations stay on the map; so do the smallest providers.
  • Sole traders with no shop sign are left out. Their address is often a home, not a shop. No name or establishment number is published here: only a position, two dates and a type.

How the map is built

For each shop we keep four things: its position, its opening month, its closing month if it closed, and its type. That makes 120,000 lines. For each frame, the page draws the shops open at that date, highlights the openings of the last three months and lets the closures of the last four fade out. Eighteen years play in about 36 seconds.

The types of shop are Pignon’s. Two types that share an activity code (bars and cafés, florists and pet shops, dry cleaners and laundromats) are merged.

And around your address?

Pignon reads the same register for one precise address: how many shops of your type within a few minutes’ walk, how many lasted three years, and the known reasons behind nearby closures. These are signals, with the number and the source behind each.
