People in public data: what publishers hide, and what we hide on top

3.2 million undisclosed companies in SIRENE, anonymised owners, directors named in one register and hidden in another: how each source protects people, why combining them is the real risk, and the rules Tesselo adds so that no profile can be built.

French public data describes companies, buildings, sales and contracts. Behind many of these rows there is a person: a craftsman registered under their own name, a company director, the owner of a flat. Each publisher has its own rules to protect them. This post goes through those rules, then explains what Tesselo does on top.

SIRENE: 3.2 million “undisclosed” companies

A sole trader can object to the distribution of their data in the SIRENE register (French Commercial Code, art. R123-232-1, which refers to GDPR art. 21). INSEE then marks the record “partial diffusion” and still publishes it in its open files, but with the name, shop sign, exact address and coordinates masked as [ND]. Still visible: the SIREN or SIRET number, the dates, the legal form, the activity, the headcount band and the commune. A company can also have the address of its head office or a site masked, but its name stays public.

So almost every hidden record is a person. INSEE’s terms of use ask reusers to take into account each record’s most recent diffusion status: a person who objects today must disappear from reuses tomorrow.

The other sources, each in its own way

  • Property sales (DVF). The tax administration publishes the price, date and address of each sale, without the parties’ names. The reuse terms forbid re-identifying people.
  • Building owners (BDNB, tax administration files). Only legal persons are named, with their SIREN. The publisher anonymises owners who are natural persons.
  • Energy performance certificates (ADEME). One certificate per dwelling, with its address and, in free text, the floor, the door or the residence name. That free text can hold a surname.
  • National company register (INPI). It names directors, natural persons included. It has no equivalent of SIRENE’s “undisclosed” status, so it can show what SIRENE hides. The register of beneficial owners has been closed to the public since July 2024, after a ruling of the Court of Justice of the European Union.
  • Annual accounts (INPI). About 45% of filings are confidential. Small companies can hide their income statement, micro companies their whole accounts.
  • Public contracts (DECP, BOAMP). Contract holders are published with their SIRET and name, sole traders included. Notices also name contact persons.
  • Food hygiene (Alim’confiance), co-ownerships (RNIC), building permits (Sitadel), classified installations (Géorisques). These files can carry the name of a craftsman or a farmer when the site is in their own name.

Each file, on its own, follows its rules. The risk comes from combining them. A SIRET hidden in SIRENE can reappear with a name in a contract notice, and a director absent from one file can be named in another. An aggregator that puts everything side by side rebuilds what each publisher took care to keep apart: the profile of a person.

What Tesselo does on top

Tesselo brings dozens of these sources into one base. So we apply stricter rules than each publisher’s, so that no data about a person is publicly accessible and no profile can be built.

  • No person’s name is stored. No director, no sole trader, no contact. When a calculation needs to match two records of one director, the name is reduced to a fingerprint in memory, then dropped: it is never written to disk.
  • Less than SIRENE for hidden records. Of an undisclosed company we keep the number only. Not the commune, not the activity, not the dates. It is found by its exact number only, never by a name, a place or a partial number, and it shows as a “company whose information is not distributed”. The status is read again at every update.
  • A hidden person has no links. For an undisclosed sole trader we show no contract, no notice, no link to another company or an address, even when another public source gives them. Putting public records side by side under one person’s number is already profiling them.
  • A business name only for a company. In every other file (public contracts, hygiene, co-ownerships, permits, classified installations), a name column is kept only when its SIREN is a legal person open in SIRENE. Otherwise it is empty.
  • Links between companies, never between people. From the INPI register we read only companies that direct other companies. Directors who are natural persons are never read, and our copy of the register is deleted once read.
  • Figures at the right scale. Property sales stay aggregated: never a single sale with its exact address and date. A certificate stays tied to the building’s address, with no floor, door or residence name. Confidential accounts stay confidential.
  • No score for a person. We rate places and companies, never a sole trader (Court of Justice of the EU, SCHUFA ruling, December 2023). A graph linking people to their companies is not built: it would first need a legal review.

These rules have a cost. Some searches get no answer, and some graphs are poorer than other services’. That is a choice: a base of public data should stay a base about places and companies, not about people.
