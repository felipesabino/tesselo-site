Paris, 2009–2025: the types of shop that rise, and those that fall back

Fewer shops selling goods, more hair, beauty and fast food, wine shops falling back since 2021: seventeen years of openings and closures by type of shop, and two reasons to read the rises with care.

From 2009 to 2025, INSEE’s SIRENE register recorded 84,038 openings and 56,983 closures in Paris for the 28 types of shop we follow. The total rises almost every year. Behind it, the types do not follow the same slope: some open twice as often as before, others now close more than they open, a few have not moved. Here is the series, year by year, and two reasons not to read it too fast.

Fewer goods, more services

In 2009, one opening in five (20.1%) was a shop that sells goods: clothes, books, toys, glasses, flowers. In 2025 it is one in eleven (9.1%), with 493 openings against 790. Hairdressers and beauty salons moved ahead in 2019: 843 openings in 2025, against 536 in 2009.

The rest changes less than one might think. Eating and drinking out (restaurants, fast food, bars and cafés, caterers) has been close to half of all openings from the start: 46.8% in 2009, 50.5% in 2025. Food shops stay at around 12 to 15% each year, except in 2021 (17.2%).

Those that close almost as often as they open

Across all types, the register counts 68 closures per 100 openings. That figure is a minimum: some closures are never declared. Its main use is to compare types with one another.

  • Clothing: 96. 7,650 closures for 7,941 openings. Over seventeen years, the type barely replaces itself.
  • Bookshops (92), dry cleaners and laundromats (90): the same picture, on smaller numbers.
  • Restaurants: 73, fast food: 62. Restaurants are the type that opens most (19,618 in seventeen years), with a ratio above the average.
  • At the bottom of the chart, nurseries, fitness studios and hobby schools. Their place owes something to the way the register is filled in: see below.

Only one type has more closures than openings over the whole period: stationers, with 553 closures for 440 openings. They are too few for the chart, like hardware stores (234 for 262) and fishmongers (133 for 159).

Clothing: the lines cross in 2019

Closures of clothing shops have changed little: between 372 and 577 a year, 372 in 2025. It is the openings that gave way: 620 in 2011, the best year, 248 in 2025, or 60% fewer. The two lines meet in 2018 (473 openings, 473 closures) and the balance turns negative in 2019, before Covid. Since 2023 the register has lost about 120 shops a year (−124, −115, −124).

The register does not say why. It only shows what registers elsewhere: across every business in Paris, including those declared at a home address, 616 online sellers registered in 2009 and 11,481 in 2025. That is one possible reading, not a proof.

Those that had their moment

Five types now open clearly less often than at their peak. For two of them, the peak was 2021.

  • Wine shops: 76 openings in 2009, 71 in 2021, 23 in 2025, the lowest of the series. Closures exceed openings in 2024 and in 2025.
  • Cheese and fine food: 124 openings in 2021, 56 in 2025. The same switch in 2024 and 2025.
  • Bikes and sports: 54 openings in 2021, 30 in 2025. The balance is negative in 2023 and in 2025.
  • Opticians: the peak is older (123 openings in 2010, 60 in 2025) and the decline steadier.

2021 is also the best year for grocery shops (437 openings), florists and pet shops (85) and nurseries (118), and the second best for wine shops. One can read in it a wave of neighbourhood projects as the lockdowns ended, which did not last for wine, cheese and bikes. That is a reading: the register gives dates, not reasons.

The balance of the last three years

Over 2023–2025, the register gains 4,129 shops (16,377 openings, 12,248 closures). Fast food alone brings 1,168 of them, more than restaurants (860). Four types are in the red: clothing (−363), wine shops (−9), florists and pet shops (−9), bookshops (−7). Cheese and fine food is just above zero (+13: 193 openings, 180 closures). Bikes and sports (−4) have fewer than 100 openings and are not in the chart.

Since closures reach the register late, a balance close to zero is probably already negative on the street.

Those that do not move

A few types open about as often every year, whatever happens.

  • Bakeries: between 133 and 171 openings a year over seventeen years. The highest year is 1.3 times the lowest. For wine shops, it is 3.3 times.
  • Butchers: between 39 and 60 openings a year.
  • Pharmacies: between 44 and 68. Their number is capped by law: an opening there is almost always a takeover.
  • Restaurants: 1,129 openings in 2009, 1,091 in 2025. Meanwhile fast food went from 588 to 1,152, and it opens more than restaurants in 2021 and in 2025.

For these types, an opening is often one operator replacing another, within the same walls. The number of premises changes little, so the number of openings does too.

Rises to read with care

Among the strongest rises of the series are caterers (49 openings in 2009, 185 in 2025), fitness studios (45, then 235), hobby schools (44, then 156) and phone repair (3, then 29). Two features of the register magnify rises of this kind. The first is removed from our figures; the second remains in them.

Business address providers are left out

A business address provider rents out an administrative address: companies declare their seat there without running a shop there. In the register, these registrations look like shops, and their weight rises fast: 109 of the 4,045 openings of 2009 (2.7%), 851 of the 6,285 openings of 2025 (13.5%).

We leave them out of every count in the blog, this post included. The rule: a street number that holds 20 registrations or more, of 10 types of shop or more, with under 20% chains. It finds 97 street numbers and 6,326 registrations, or 4.8% of the 131,105 we started from. The busiest holds 763, of 24 different types.

The effect depends on the type. In 2025 these addresses received 140 of the 325 caterer openings (43%), 25 of the 54 phone repair openings, 69 of the 304 fitness studios (23%), 148 of the 676 beauty salons (22%) and 196 of the 1,348 fast food places (15%). For restaurants it was 37 out of 1,128 (3%). With them, caterer openings were multiplied by 6 between 2009 and 2025; without them, by 3.8.

The rule has two limits. It keeps shopping centres and railway stations, where the shops exist: 48 street numbers hold 20 registrations or more without being removed (1,331 registrations, 24% of them chains). And it does not see small address providers, under the thresholds: 95 street numbers hold 10 to 19 registrations of 7 types or more, with under 20% chains. The register does not say which of them are administrative addresses. Together they receive 51 of the 5,434 openings of 2025 (0.9%).

Since 2021, more sole traders declare a shop sign

We leave out sole traders without a shop sign: their address is most often a home. Yet between 2020 and 2021, the share of new registrations that declare a sign goes from 8.4% to 33% for cultural teaching, and from 16% to 22.9% for every business in Paris. We do not know the cause. The jump affects so many activities at once that it looks like a change in the way the register is filled in, not a change on the street.

The consequence is direct. Our hobby schools go from 94 openings in 2020 to 206 in 2021. Over the same year, total registrations under that activity code, with or without a sign, go from 346 to 455, and the total is lower in 2025 (304) than in 2016 (538). For fitness studios and sports lessons, the total goes from 280 registrations in 2009 to 500 in 2025: almost double, far from the 45 then 235 openings of our series.

Hairdressers do not show this jump: 41.6% of new registrations declare a sign in 2020, 43.6% in 2021. Nor do beauty salons (41.5%, then 37.5%).

What these figures do not say

  • A date in a register is not a cause. The readings offered here (online selling, post-lockdown projects, business address providers) are hypotheses.
  • Closures reach the register late, and sometimes never. Balances are therefore too favourable, above all for recent years.
  • A takeover or a move counts as one closure and one opening. A type with a stable number of shops can show a lot of movement.
  • A registration is not a shop window. Address providers too small for our rule, kitchens with no dining room, work done at the customer’s home: the register counts them like the others.
  • The type comes from the activity code the operator declares. It can be imprecise, and several trades share one code.
  • Small numbers move a lot. The charts keep only the types with at least 500 openings over the period, or 100 over three years.
  • The thresholds are ours: 20 registrations, 10 types and 20% chains to remove an address, 60% of the peak to speak of a fall-back. Other thresholds would move a few lines, not the overall picture.
