Is the shop on the map still open? We asked three sources

The SIRENE register learns about closures late. Checked against OpenStreetMap, up to 78% of the shops of one type have no trace on the map; APUR’s street survey finds 20 to 42% of them open. What each source sees, and why “not on the map” does not mean “closed”.

INSEE’s SIRENE register says which shops are open. But it learns about closures late: a shop that shuts without any formality stays “active” for months, sometimes years. To find these ghosts, we checked the register against two other sources: the OpenStreetMap map and the shop survey of the Paris urban planning agency (APUR). What they say together is more nuanced than what each says alone.

Three sources, three ways of seeing a shop

  • SIRENE (INSEE) records declarations: an opening, a closure, a change of activity. Nothing forces anyone to declare a de facto closure quickly.
  • OpenStreetMap is a map made by volunteers. A shop is on it if someone saw it and added it, and leaves it if someone removes it.
  • APUR’s survey (BDCOM) lists every ground-floor premises in Paris, street by street, every three years: 60,845 premises in 2023, with the sign seen on the shopfront.

The rule: not on the map, so maybe closed

A shop that is open in the register, opened more than a year ago, with no matching place in OpenStreetMap and no unmatched place within 25 metres, is marked “may have closed”. We do not remove it: it counts as half a competitor.

The rates are high everywhere. For convenience stores and grocers in Paris and its inner ring, 46% are flagged (2,503 of 5,401), and 55% of corner shops in the strict sense (code 47.11B), against 19% of supermarkets. Undeclared closures do not explain it all. Many registrations are not shopfronts: an online seller, an importer, a dormant company. And many real shops are simply not on the map.

The third witness: people who walked the street

To decide, we looked for every flagged shop in APUR’s survey: same address, an activity of the same type, a single sign, registered before the 2023 survey.

  • Between 20 and 42% of flagged shops were in fact open for most types. These are real shops that nobody added to the map. We lifted the flag for 5,762 shops.
  • For pet shops, barely 2%. Here the flag mostly means “not mapped”: OpenStreetMap knows only 66 pet shops in Paris, and the activity code also covers florists. APUR’s survey does not settle it either, because its own categories match that code poorly.
  • The effect on our scores is small: under one point on average after the check, six at most. The ranking of neighbourhoods hardly changes.

What to take away

  • “Not on the map” does not mean “closed”. The flag is a clue, not a verdict. We show it as one.
  • The register overstates what is open, and so also how long shops survive, since a shop closed without a formality counts as having lasted.
  • No source is enough alone. The register knows who is declared, the map what a volunteer saw, the survey what was in the shopfront three years ago. Combining them gives a better picture, never a certain one.
  • APUR’s survey exists only in Paris. Elsewhere in France, read the flag with even more care.
